Backtest

Each season from 2004 to 2026, the model predicts every team's point differential using only what was known before that season, then gets scored on the real result. Lower error is better. Draft classes count only after five seasons have passed, and trades only after three, so nothing leaks from the future.

How it works

  1. Player value. Wins above replacement from a blend of VORP and Win Shares, from Basketball Reference.
  2. Contracts. Pay is measured as a share of that year's salary cap, so inflation does not distort 1991 against 2024. Surplus is wins produced minus the wins that salary buys.
  3. Draft. Each pick is compared with what that slot normally returns over five seasons, scaled by how much that slot varies. A 30th pick who hits scores higher than a first pick who hits.
  4. Trades. Players and picks are valued by their next three seasons. Picks are valued by the player later selected.
  5. Free agency and fringe signings. First-season surplus of free agents, and production from two-way, Exhibit 10 and 10-day signees credited to the team that signed them first.
  6. Results. Point differential and playoff rounds, with half a point for the play-in since 2021.
  7. Known gaps. Salary data stops after 2024, so 2025 and 2026 contract scores are neutral. Penalty years for two 76ers picks are as reported. The Clippers' five forfeited firsts are in 2029–33 and not yet scored.
Data: Basketball Reference. Updated automatically each day.
